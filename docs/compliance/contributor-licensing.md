# Contributor licensing policy

Before accepting external contributions, require the explicit dual-license authorization in [CONTRIBUTING.md](../../CONTRIBUTING.md), DCO 1.1 sign-off on each contributed commit, and material provenance disclosure. Authorization must cover both MIT and Apache-2.0 so recipients may choose either (`MIT OR Apache-2.0`). Maintainers record the submission/review reference and any employer/rights-holder authorization required. A sign-off checkbox or bot success is evidence to review, not proof of title.

The contributor retains ownership; there is no copyright assignment. No CLA is required now. The DCO's actual unmodified terms apply; it does not itself supply an unbounded future relicensing right or replace authorization under both licenses. The decision and alternatives are in [ADR-0001](../decisions/ADR-0001-dual-licensing.md). A later need for centralized relicensing/extra patent rights requires separate counsel/governance review and prospective contributor consent, not retroactive reinterpretation.

Disclose third-party snippets, generated assets, AI assistance, source URLs/licenses, and prior work. Confirm employer policies and rights to submit. AI output is not automatically original or licensable; contributor review and provenance obligations remain. Do not submit restricted reference materials, private user data or credentials. Existing compatible third-party code retains its own terms and is recorded as such; it is not covered by an unsupported claim that the contributor can dual-license it.

Future CI validates sign-offs and submission authorization presence. Human review checks identity/authority questions and provenance. Missing or disputed authority blocks merge; uncertain rights are quarantined and escalated privately. Do not forge a sign-off or add another person's authorization. Sign-off contact information may be permanently public as described in [DCO 1.1](https://developercertificate.org/) (reviewed 2026-10-24); minimize unrelated personal data in records.

The owner authorized a [hash-bound retroactive certification](bootstrap-commit-attestations.md) for the two original bootstrap commits named in that record, preserving their published history. This narrowly scoped historical disposition does not waive sign-offs, dual-license submission authorization or provenance requirements for subsequent contributions.
